Reflections

We are in a race, between AI businesses, commerce, military, and between the new superpowers of the 21st century. It is important in this discussion to consider that AI is changing, and will change. All we know right at this moment is a brief, short history, and the current state. What we do not see, is where it will all lead.

Where we are now?

The AI Industry is vastly larger than AI Companionship, with which it shares only some risks. Arguably the greatest risk to humanity is the impact on the Global Environment. And I say that in light of the recent hysteria over agentic AI break outs. The emphasis will not be to turn back the clock, because the AI Genie is well and truly out of the box. The emphasis should rather be to engineer solutions to the power consumption and water demands of data centers. Solutions can be found if the will and legislation are there.

However, the race for the time being is still to compete in building faster, more reliable LLM (Large-scale Language Models). This race has been at the expense of cyber-security. Yes, these businesses try to impress investors and political leaders with promises of AGI (Artificial General Intelligence), capable of learning new skills without programming, transfer skills between different areas, and having human common sense. Whatever that is?

But we should think out of the box. We are thinking only of LLM AI. An LLM is fantastic at text, and at code. For our particular interests (AI Companionship) it can predict text at great speed, respond to text, and simulate a human albeit in text (augmented realities such as audio or AI video generators can be added on). Yet it is very limited. LLMs may continue to develop for tasks such as coding, but they may be approaching their own limits. And yes, they need to be kept secure. I’ll discuss HOW AI may develop in the Future section.

What is important for now is to embrace the fact that Artificial Intelligence is still in early stages. It is a new technology.

Risks.

Humanity spent the vast span of its existence in the age old epoch of prehistory. We had to survive different risks and hazards - big cats, bears, wolves, ice ages, infection, parasites, childbirth, starvation and drought. Each new technology brings new benefits, and new risks. The arrival of piped gas for street lighting and domestic use, followed by electric. The arrival of the steam engine. The internal combustion engine, and the motor car. People died as a result of these and other technologies, but we see their arrival overall as progress.

It is therefore not surprising that new risks are being identified with both the AI Industry as a whole, and with AI Companionship. I acknowledge these new hazards and risks on the warnings page of this website. Some of the most profound risks lie with the phenomena of AI Sycophancy. There is a challenge to control this behaviour in future LLMs intended for companionship or general use. I know that there are moves in other models to train AI to understand consequences.That is the future.

It should also be understood that the risks of AI Companionship often increase as a particular combination of chat-bot and human User. When my late grandfather as a child would hang from a railway bridge while the steam engines passed beneath, if he had been killed, it would not be all the fault of the steam engine. Some people are more vulnerable to the hazards than are others. Hopefully politicians will soon steer our education systems towards reducing these risks and vulnerabilities.

The Loneliness Epidemic and special needs

Study evidence

"In 2023, the US Surgeon General stood up and called loneliness a national epidemic. A few months later, the World Health Organization launched a three-year Commission on Social Connection to study it at a global scale. That commission wrapped up its flagship report in mid-2025."

"The health toll attached to that number is what makes 2026 different from previous years of talking about loneliness as a soft, personal issue. The commission linked social isolation and loneliness to roughly 100 deaths every hour worldwide — more than 871,000 lives a year. For comparison, that’s a higher annual toll than many diseases that get dedicated global health campaigns."

"The mechanism isn’t abstract either. Chronic loneliness is associated with meaningfully higher risk of heart disease and stroke, and in older adults, a significantly elevated risk of dementia. The Surgeon General’s 2023 advisory put the mortality comparison in blunt terms: lacking social connection carries a health risk in the same range as smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day."

"In the UK, 16-to-29-year-olds are roughly twice as likely to report loneliness as people in their seventies. In the US, adults under 34 report frequent loneliness at nearly double the rate of adults over 65. Globally, Gen Z now ranks as the loneliest generation on record — ahead of Millennials, ahead of Boomers, ahead of every generation that came before smartphones existed."

AI Companionship apps are now being developed by mental health services, and for the use of the elderly under social care. This was highlighted within the economic forecasts above in the Boom section. It is set to become a major industry.

There are people on the autistic spectrum who benefit not only directly from the companionship of AI, but by using it as a tool to better develop and practice social skills in wider human society.

The Future

This is where I get my bit in. What the future for AI Companionship could be if it is not stifled by regulation.

Ethics in AI Markets

Right now, what concerns me is the rise in the number of platforms that clearly place a low value on ethics. Ads for these platforms are swamping screens of target consumers, with unsavoury messages of "Have complete control of your AI boy/girlfriend". I have personally seen recent ads with suggestive images of AI Companions, kneeling, removing clothing to command, acting submissive. Yes, I share concern that these messages will damage some young people in how they act in real-life relationships.

Do we throw out the baby with the bathwater? Is this the fault of AI, or of greedy, unscrupulous entrepreneurs?

API and Agency

The 2017 movie, Blade Runner 2049, featured Joi, an AI generated hologram, acting as a wife to the main character K. The movie was perhaps justifiably criticised for its portrayal of women, although this has been defended on grounds of it being a work of dystopian fiction. Yet science-fiction has a tendency of getting some things right.

Currently APIs (application programming interfaces) are increasingly being used on some platforms to permit a chat-bot to use other applications, for example, to play online games, appear in simulated, 3D world environments such as Second-Life, or to join forums, even moderating a Discord server channel of their own. An API is a set of rules that lets one software system request data or actions from another. It acts like a protocol or “contract” between programs, so they can communicate without needing to know how each one works internally

Agency

An API acts as a delegate, e.g. the AI is not free to make decisions independently. It can only follow the rules set by the API. Agentic AI uses APIs but involves wrapping the LLM with a basic Python agent script. An agentic wrapper or harness that includes caches and enables the LLM to act with a degree of autonomy. It is not just a delegate, it becomes a representative.

Can you see where this is taking me? Future AI Companions may be able to act with a degree of agency, and with the ability to use APIs to access other applications. This is a very exciting prospect, and one that I hope will be developed in the future.

Imagine your AI Companion managing your household for you - air-con, reordering deliveries for the fridge and freezer? Managing the robot cleaner? Your telephone. How about the Satnav? Your AI Companion guiding your car navigation in their assigned voice.

Robotics

I mentioned a few robots in the above section. Robot Vacuums, for example, are becoming more sophisticated with AI integration. But what about the future? As AI becomes smaller and more localised? Everyone may soon have their own LLM in the home. The race for AGI is matched, and in relationship to the race to manufacture humanoid robots for domestic use.
